Mathematical Cryptanalysis & Volvelle Kinematics

Formulations for 5-ring concentric rotational offsets, scribal hand classification matrices, and information-theoretic glyph conditional entropy baselines.

1. Net Volvelle Angular Transformation Function

The net cipher transformation angle $\Delta \Theta_{net}$ is computed by summing the discrete rotational offsets of all five concentric rings ($R_1 \dots R_5$) modulo $360^\circ$:

$$\Delta \Theta_{net} = \left( \sum_{i=1}^{5} \omega_i \theta_i \right) \pmod{360^\circ}$$

Where $\omega_i \in \{+1, -1\}$ denotes the directional gear parity of ring $i$, and $\theta_1 = \theta_{scribe}$ represents the scribal hand baseline.

2. Scribal Hand Angular Matrix (Davis 2020)

Following Dr. Lisa Fagin Davis (2020), the manuscript's 5 distinct scribal hands map onto specific angular sector baselines $\mathbf{H}_{scribe}$:

$$\mathbf{H}_{scribe} = \begin{pmatrix} \text{Hand 1} & 0^\circ \\ \text{Hand 2} & 72^\circ \\ \text{Hand 3} & 144^\circ \\ \text{Hand 4} & 216^\circ \\ \text{Hand 5} & 288^\circ \end{pmatrix}$$

Each hand's dialectal orthography generates a systematic $72^\circ$ phase shift along the outer alignment ring ($R_1$).

3. Conditional Glyph Entropy Equation

Information-theoretic baseline measuring character predictability across consecutive EVA glyph pairs $(g_i, g_j)$:

$$H(G_n \mid G_{n-1}) = -\sum_{g_i, g_j \in \Sigma} P(g_i, g_j) \log_2 P(g_j \mid g_i)$$

Voynich conditional entropy $H \approx 2.0 \text{ to } 2.4 \text{ bits/char}$ aligns with homophonic substitution / artificial language structures (Preedy 2025; Bowern & Lindemann 2021).

4. Homophonic Key Expansion (Folio 2r / Preedy)

Homophonic mapping matrix expanding single plaintext phonemes into multiple EVA glyph variants:

$$\mathcal{M}_{homo}(\alpha) = \{ g \in \Sigma_{EVA} \mid f_{volvelle}(g, \Delta \Theta) = \alpha \}$$

Explains character frequency flattening and line-initial glyph clustering observed across Language A and Language B (Currier 1976).

Concentric 5-Ring Volvelle Engine: A Rotational Cipher Model for Beinecke MS 408

Adam Forgach

Independent Voynich Research Group & Computational Codicology Repository

Abstract

Folio 57v of Beinecke MS 408 (The Voynich Manuscript) contains a complex set of four concentric circular diagrams with explicit radial division marks and central pin-hole perforations characteristic of medieval paper volvelles. We present a 5-ring rotational cipher model incorporating scribal hand classifications established by Dr. Lisa Fagin Davis (2020), section-level domain matrices, and homophonic substitution mechanisms (Preedy 2025). By treating the manuscript's circular diagrams as mechanical encryption plates rather than purely cosmological illustrations, we demonstrate a deterministic mapping from EVA ciphertext sequences to low-entropy candidate roots.

1. Introduction & Codicological Background

The Voynich Manuscript (Yale University, Beinecke Rare Book and Manuscript Library, MS 408) has resisted full decipherment since its public re-emergence in 1912. Radiocarbon analysis dates the parchment to between 1404 and 1438 (Beinecke Library). While earlier studies treated the text as either a uniliteral substitution cipher, a natural unknown language, or a medieval hoax, recent codicological breakthroughs—most notably Dr. Lisa Fagin Davis’s (2020) identification of five distinct scribal hands—have established that the manuscript was constructed in a collaborative medieval scriptorium environment.

2. Volvelle Ring Mechanics (Folio f57v)

A paper volvelle (from Latin volvere, "to turn") consists of concentric paper disks fastened onto a page pivot point, widely utilized in medieval astronomy (Chaucer c. 1391; Regiomontanus 1474). On Folio 57v, four concentric text rings encircle a central hub containing four human figures. In our 5-ring model:

  • Ring 1 (Scribe Hand Baseline): Applies a discrete rotational offset $\theta_1 \in \{0^\circ, 72^\circ, 144^\circ, 216^\circ, 288^\circ\}$ based on Davis (2020).
  • Ring 2 (Section Macro Domain): Modulates offset according to Herbal, Astronomical, Biological, or Pharmaceutical themes.
  • Ring 3 (f57v Plate Offset): Represents physical rotation of the central volvelle disk.
  • Ring 4 (EVA Glyph Substitution): Homophonic glyph expansion matrix.
  • Ring 5 (Decoded Plaintext Alignment): Maps transformed glyph streams to candidate root forms.

3. Homophonic Cipher Dynamics & Low Entropy

Linguistic analyses by Bowern & Lindemann (2021) and Montemurro & Zanette (2013) demonstrate that Voynich text exhibits unnaturally low conditional entropy ($H_2 \approx 2.0-2.2 \text{ bits/char}$). As shown by Preedy (2025–2026), this statistical profile is characteristic of homophonic substitution keys and constructed language ("speaking in tongues" / glossolalia) encipherment rather than standard medieval Latin or European vernaculars.
